## Copy-ready master prompt

Create a 24-second photorealistic automotive studio film of the exact Pagani Huayra BC in the supplied identity reference, presented as ONE UNINTERRUPTED TAKE. One car, one camera, one continuous white infinity-cove environment. The camera physically travels between viewpoints; the film never edits between separate compositions. Preserve the exact car geometry, selected livery, stripe placement, wheel design, interior and number 20 throughout.

The car stays in one fixed position and orientation on the floor. Wheels remain stationary until they detach during the final exploded assembly. All doors and body panels remain closed during the camera tour. Use one fixed focal length throughout. The camera may change height and distance only through visible, smooth physical movement. Maintain consistent studio lighting, exposure and white balance, with natural changes in reflections as the viewpoint changes. Keep the entire car comfortably inside the frame at every angle, with room for the later raised and detached components.

Follow this continuous choreography. The timings are milestones within ONE take, not separate shots:

0–3 seconds: begin at the supplied front three-quarter view. Glide smoothly toward the car's left-side profile, showing every intermediate viewpoint. If the starting reference reveals the other flank, travel around the nose continuously to reach the left side; never mirror or flip the vehicle.

3–6 seconds: continue the same orbit around the rear, through a clear rear view, to the right-side profile. Show the actual rear passage connecting the two sides. No camera teleport, vehicle rotation or direction jump.

6–9 seconds: from the right side, follow a smooth ascending crane path over the roof. Gradually tilt the camera downward until the complete closed car is visible from directly overhead. Preserve a stable camera orientation without sudden roll or an upside-down image. The ascent and perspective change must be visible continuously.

9–12 seconds: continue along a smooth curved camera path down from overhead to an elevated rear-left three-quarter view. Gently ease to a stationary camera position. The vehicle remains in exactly the same floor position and orientation. Do not cut from overhead to rear view.

12–15 seconds: hold this established rear camera pose while both doors swing smoothly upward on their existing hinges. Show their complete movement. No new doors appear, and no body panels change shape.

15–18 seconds: doors stay fully open. The rear engine clamshell lifts smoothly on its existing hinges, exposing the engine. Preserve the existing wing and panel relationships during this opening motion. The camera stays in the same rear three-quarter pose.

18–23 seconds: seamlessly enter an elegant exploded-assembly visualization of THIS SAME CAR. Start from the exact open-door, open-engine pose already on screen. The carbon chassis remains the anchor at the original vehicle position. The exterior upper body assembly lifts vertically, preserving the raised doors and open clamshell pose. The rear wing detaches and moves upward and backward. The existing engine rises out of its bay. Exactly four existing wheel-and-tire assemblies move outward from their hubs along the axle directions. Stagger these motions gently and leave clear air gaps between the eight main groups: upper body assembly, wing, engine, chassis and four wheels. Every module visibly travels from its own original location; nothing materializes, duplicates, dissolves, changes scale or swaps to a different drawing. Keep the same materials, lighting, perspective and selected color. Use restrained controlled motion, with no debris, flying fasteners or violent explosion. A slow straight camera dolly backward is allowed only if needed to keep the entire assembly inside the frame; no zoom or camera-angle change.

23–24 seconds: hold the complete exploded assembly, with every component visible and stable. Do not return to the front or reset the animation.

The result should feel like a continuous premium automotive visualization filmed by one robotic camera. Changing viewpoint is achieved through camera travel, not editing. No cuts, crossfades, dissolves, ghosted double images, transitions between still photographs, object morphs, lens changes, abrupt speed changes, turntable rotation, mirrored car, drifting decals, duplicate numbers, missing wheels, extra components, cropped parts, people, labels, captions or watermark. The number 20 remains fixed to the same panel from start to finish. Clean 16:9 composition; audio is not needed.
